# Endmyopia trims glasses, not eyeball length

Jake Steiner's Endmyopia, also sold as the "Reduced Lens Method", tells people with low to moderate myopia to stop wearing full-strength glasses. Instead they use **two deliberately weakened pairs**. "Normalized" glasses for distance sit about **0.25 D under the 20/20 correction**. "Differentials" for screens and close work sit about **1–2 D under full**, set by the screen distance. Full correction is kept for **night driving**. The method adds "active focus" on slight blur and steps lenses down by **0.25 D every 3–4 months**, a pace the site says yields about **1 D a year**. The pieces with real support are not new. Outdoor time prevents myopia in children. Pseudomyopia and over-minus prescriptions are real. Myopic defocus does thicken the choroid. But the effects measured in the lab are **micron-scale and fade within hours**, worth roughly 0.03–0.06 D. No controlled trial has tested the protocol. Endmyopia's own site now concedes that **adult axial-length reversal "has not been demonstrated in a trial"**. Its self-reported registry shows a median gain of about **1.75 D from about −5 D**, and almost none of those cases were measured with cycloplegic refraction or axial length. The likeliest explanation for most reported gains is relaxed focusing (pseudomyopia), blur adaptation and a starting prescription that was slightly too strong. Shrinking eyeballs are not the explanation. For a careful adult with low to moderate myopia, the physical risk is modest if they keep a legal driving correction and keep having exams. The method is a poor fit for children. It also does nothing to lower the retinal and glaucoma risks, because those follow eyeball length, not lens power.

*Transparency note: this is a research synthesis, not medical advice. Endmyopia.org, its wiki, Reddit, Trustpilot, Quora, PubMed Central and several journal sites were blocked from direct fetching in the research environment. Many findings, especially Endmyopia's exact wording, prices and registry figures, therefore come from search-engine snippets of the cited pages taken on 3 October 2026, not full reads. Some Endmyopia wiki pages are community-edited and not always Steiner's own words. Re-check exact figures against the original pages before relying on them.*

## Steiner prescribes two reduced pairs, plus full correction for night driving

Endmyopia's wiki is blunt about the number of pairs. **"You only need two reduced pairs of glasses – don't wear loads of different pairs of normalised and differentials for different focal distances."** It also says **"Normalized are worn for all distance vision activities (except night driving), while differentials are worn exclusively for close-up work with a fixed focal distance"** ([Endmyopia Wiki – FAQ/FFAQ](https://wiki.endmyopia.org/index.php?title=Frequently_Asked_Questions%2FFFAQ&mobileaction=toggle_view_desktop)).

Full correction is kept for the dark: **"When driving at night, it is strongly recommended that you wear full correction glasses, that are more powerful than your normalized"** ([Endmyopia Wiki – Night vision](https://wiki.endmyopia.org/wiki/Night_vision)).

For someone who never drives at night, the answer is **two pairs**: normalized and differentials. A third, full-correction pair is recommended for night driving. For non-night-drivers it is only **inferred to be optional**, because no source found says outright that non-night-drivers can skip it. In practice the old full-strength glasses cost nothing to keep. They are also the safest pair for daytime driving whenever the normalized pair might fall short of the local legal standard. Check your jurisdiction's driving vision rules: the UK requires about 6/12 (≈20/40) plus reading a number plate at 20 m ([AOP – Driving and vision standards](https://www.aop.org.uk/advice-and-support/for-patients/driving-and-vision-standards)).

|---|---|---|---|

### Worked example: a −4.00 D myope at a 65 cm screen

The differential formula puts the screen right at the edge of clear vision. A screen at 65 cm corresponds to 100 ÷ 65 ≈ **1.54 D**. Subtracting that from −4.00 gives −2.46, which rounds to the nearest stock lens step of **−2.50 D**. Through −2.50 the eye is left with 1.50 D of uncorrected myopia, so text is clear out to about 67 cm and blurs just beyond the screen. That matches Endmyopia's "blur horizon" instruction to **"minimize the prescription to where you can only see as far as your comfortable ergonomic viewing distance… Beyond that primary distance, you want to encounter some blur"** ([Defined: The Blur Horizon](https://endmyopia.org/frauenfeld-method-key-concepts-the-blur-horizon/)).

Steiner's generic 1.25 D reduction would give −2.75. That pair is clear to about 80 cm and leaves more margin, and it suits people who drift back from the screen. The wiki's upper bound of "2.00 under" would give −2.00, which is clear only to about 50 cm. That would blur a 65 cm screen, which shows why the distance-based calculation matters more than the headline range. The arithmetic here is this report's illustration, not Endmyopia text.

| Pair for a −4.00 D myope | Power | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Normalized | **−3.75** (or −3.50 if −4.00 is stronger than needed for 20/20) | 0.25 D under 20/20; no more than 0.5 D under full |
| Differentials, by formula | **−2.50** | 100/65 ≈ 1.54 D, rounded to the 0.25 D lens step |
| Differentials, Steiner default | −2.75 | 1.25 D reduction. More conservative; clear to about 80 cm |
| Full correction | −4.00 | Night driving; optional otherwise, subject to local driving law |

Endmyopia sequences the change rather than swapping both pairs at once. One page title spells out the order, ["Differential Now, Normalized In 4-6 Weeks"](https://endmyopia.org/pro-topic-differential-now-normalized-in-4-6-weeks/).

### Step-downs, astigmatism and high myopes follow slower, narrower rules

After the initial switch, users are told to **"alternate between differentials and normalized with 4-6 weeks of adjustment period between changes"** ([Wiki – Normalized](https://wiki.endmyopia.org/wiki/Normalized)). The typical pace is **"roughly a quarter-diopter every 3-4 months, which amounts to about a diopter a year."** On a plateau, users are told to give it "an extra month at the current prescription where there's no notable change, then [take] a step down regardless" ([How Fast Can I Improve My Vision?](https://endmyopia.org/how-fast-can-i-improve-my-vision/)). Readiness is judged at home with Snellen charts and a centimetre test (diopters = 100 ÷ the farthest distance in cm at which text stays sharp) ([Wiki – Measurement](https://wiki.endmyopia.org/index.php?title=Measurement&mobileaction=toggle_view_desktop)). As discussed below, this is exactly the measurement that blur adaptation distorts.

Astigmatism is handled one variable at a time. **"If sphere is being reduced, cylinder should not be changed at the same time, and conversely."** Cylinder of 0.25 D "can be dropped, with no other changes". The **maximum cylinder reduction in close-up glasses is 1 D**, and "a more conservative drop would be half diopter (which is about equivalent of 0.25 diopters spherical)". The axis is not changed if it has been stable ([Wiki – Astigmatism](https://wiki.endmyopia.org/wiki/Astigmatism)). For our example myope with, say, −4.00 −0.75 × 180, that means reducing sphere first and touching cylinder only in a later, separate step.

High myopes get bigger steps but less freedom. **"If your myopia is higher (7 diopter+)… you may consider doing a 0.5 D reduction (0.75 D from full correction)."** "No-correction" days become days in differentials instead ([Wiki – Reduction](https://wiki.endmyopia.org/wiki/Reduction)). Yet the site describes the method as being for **"low to moderate nearsightedness"** ([About Endmyopia](https://endmyopia.org/about-endmyopia/)). High myopes are also exactly the group whose long eyes carry the greatest retinal risk.

## A self-described ex-trader built a free method with a paid upsell

By his own account, Steiner is a former banking and stock-trading professional who retired early, "entirely eliminated his −5 diopter myopia", and has "helped over 1.2 million myopes" ([About: Jake Steiner](https://endmyopia.org/about-us/)). Podcast bios stress that he is **"not a medical doctor or optometrist"** and that his advice is **"100% unlicensed, unsanctioned, un-endorsed by the optometry establishment"** ([Finding Genius Podcast](https://futuretech.findinggeniuspodcast.com/podcasts/vision-quest-jake-steiner-founder-of-endmyopia-org-the-way-to-reverse-myopia-take-your-vision-back/)). Elsewhere the branding inflates this: his YouTube channel calls him **"a myopia control scientist with 20 years of experience"** ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCEhYxefHylpSgoiXDWcBrlA)). No degree, publication or licence behind that title was found. Critics on Quora say "Jake Steiner" is a pseudonym ([Quora](https://www.quora.com/Is-endmymyopia-orgs-information-true-Can-myopia-really-be-reversible)). That claim is unverified, and so is the rest of his biography, including his own before-and-after refraction.

The core method is free. A subscription program, **"BackTo20/20"**, sells structure and support. Snippets list tiers from **$77 to $289 a month**, a $1 trial, and lifetime options around $740–$1,330 ([BackTo20/20](https://join.endmyopia.org/)). An earlier Core course was quoted at $1,450 ([Consultations](https://endmyopia.org/consultations/)). The site also discloses that Steiner **owns stock in lens manufacturers** ([About: Jake Steiner](https://endmyopia.org/about-us/)). His rhetoric is openly hostile to the profession: he has called optometry "a $100 billion industry that feeds on people's ignorance" ([MelanieAvalon.com](https://melanieavalon.com/myopia/)). Endmyopia says Steiner **"retired from active publishing in 2025"**, while the community and paid program continue ([About Endmyopia](https://endmyopia.org/about-endmyopia/)). Recent pages read far more cautiously than the "Get Rid Of Glasses" homepage headline ([endmyopia.org](https://endmyopia.org/)), which suggests other hands may now be shaping the content. That suggestion is unconfirmed.

## Endmyopia's claims outrun its own registry

Endmyopia's headline claims are large. It says Steiner fully reversed −5 D, that 1.2 million people were helped, and that **"around 1 diopter per year is a realistic rate"** for consistent users ([How Do You Improve Myopia?](https://endmyopia.org/how-do-you-improve-myopia/)). The site's best evidence is a self-published Case Report Registry. In one snapshot it lists **363 cases from 2013–2026** with a **median start of about −5.00 D and a median reduction of about −1.75 D**. Of these, **61** carry some third-party signal: 49 reported optometrist or ophthalmologist results, and 12 driving-licence restrictions lifted ([Case Report Registry](https://endmyopia.org/myopia-improvement-registry/)). Another snippet of the same page gives **289 distinct cases with 42 verified**. The discrepancy is unresolved and may reflect different snapshots. Either way, the registry is candid about its limits. It is "self-reported, subject to selection and survivorship bias… It does not prove that the method reliably reverses established adult myopia" ([Case Report Registry](https://endmyopia.org/myopia-improvement-registry/)).

The registry undercuts the marketing in three ways. First, 363 cases are three orders of magnitude short of "1.2 million helped", so that figure reads as audience reach. Second, the median participant still wears roughly −3.25 D, which is far from "BackTo20/20". Third, even the verified cases rest on routine non-cycloplegic prescriptions or acuity-only driving tests. Neither can separate a structural change from relaxed focusing. Endmyopia's own explainer names the test that would settle it: cycloplegic drops reveal pseudomyopia ([Pseudomyopia vs axial myopia](https://endmyopia.org/what-is-the-difference-between-pseudomyopia-and-axial-myopia/)). Yet no registry case with before-and-after cycloplegic refraction and optical biometry was found. The single showcased biometry case reports **0.08–0.10 mm** of axial reduction over a year, with device and conditions unstated ([Axial Length Reduced](https://endmyopia.org/axial-length-reduced-biometry-results/)). Change of that size sits within normal choroidal and measurement variation.

The method's stated mechanism also shifts. The wiki says pseudomyopia ("ciliary spasm") "becomes lens-induced myopia if you start wearing glasses" ([Wiki – FAQ/Myopia](https://wiki.endmyopia.org/wiki/Frequently_Asked_Questions/Myopia)). It says hyperopic defocus from full-strength lenses at near drives elongation, and that "without differentials, you're on the path to ever increasing prescriptions" ([Wiki – Differentials](https://wiki.endmyopia.org/index.php?title=Differentials&mobileaction=toggle_view_desktop)). Yet the 2026 site states plainly: **"Axial length reversal in adults has not been demonstrated in a trial… The documented mechanism shows growth is regulated by visual input, not that adult human myopia has been reversed"** ([Can myopia be reversed?](https://endmyopia.org/can-myopia-be-reversed/)).

| Endmyopia claims | What independent evidence shows |
|---|---|
| About 1 D a year of improvement for consistent users | No controlled trial exists. Registry median is about 1.75 D total, self-reported and survivor-biased |
| Full-strength lenses at near cause "lens-induced myopia" | Hyperopic defocus lengthens eyes in animals and in short-term human measurements. Reduced-near-power lenses slow progression modestly in **children**. Unproven in adults |
| Reducing lenses lets the eye recover | Myopic defocus shortens measured axial length by **~7–20 µm, transiently** |
| Clear flashes and active focus train the eye | No peer-reviewed study of "clear flashes". Acuity gains are explained by blur adaptation and accommodative release |
| Steiner reversed −5 D; 1.2 million helped | Self-reported; no clinical records published; registry holds 289–363 cases |
| Wiki: myopic defocus "has been shown to reduce axial length in a clinical study" | Site's own 2026 page: adult reversal "has not been demonstrated in a trial" |

## Lab science supports micron-scale defocus effects, not reversal

Most myopia is axial: the eyeball grows too long. The kernel of truth in Endmyopia is that the eye does respond to defocus. In young adults, 40–60 minutes of +3 D myopic defocus **decreased measured axial length by 8–13 µm**, and an hour of +2 D defocus thickened the choroid by about **20 µm** ([IOVS](https://iovs.arvojournals.org/article.aspx?articleid=2793556)). A time-course study found changes of about **±10 µm at 60 minutes** that receded once defocus ended ([Sci Rep 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-65151-5)). Optical biometers measure to the retinal pigment layer, so a thicker choroid reads as a "shorter" eye even though the outer wall (the sclera) has not moved ([Myopia Profile](https://www.myopiaprofile.com/articles/is-axial-length-regression-possible)). The standard rule of thumb is that 1 mm of axial length ≈ 2.5–3 D. On that basis these effects are worth roughly **0.03–0.06 D**, below a single 0.25 D lens step. Even red-light therapy, the most aggressive intervention studied, moved adult axial length only **−0.06 mm in four weeks**, alongside an 18 µm choroidal thickening ([RLRL adult RCT](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9780890/)). Red-light devices also face safety concerns: commercial units were found to exceed laser-safety limits in 2026 testing ([Optometry Times](https://www.optometrytimes.com/view/some-red-light-therapy-devices-may-exceed-safety-levels-for-myopia-control-study-suggests)).

Better-documented phenomena explain the reported gains without any eye shortening.

**Blur adaptation.** Twenty-two young myopes went three hours without glasses. Their uncorrected acuity improved from **0.76 to 0.53 logMAR** with **no change in refractive error**. The gain was perceptual, a brain adaptation ([SUNY – Blur adaptation in myopes](https://researchconnect.suny.edu/en/publications/blur-adaptation-in-myopes/)). This is the exact confounder of home Snellen tracking.

**Pseudomyopia and over-minus prescriptions.** In Norwegian adults aged 18–40, about **7% met criteria for pseudomyopia**. Non-cycloplegic autorefraction read on average **0.43 D more myopic**, and **40%** of people read at least 0.50 D more myopic without the drops ([USN Open Archive](https://openarchive.usn.no/usn-xmlui/handle/11250/2725703)).

**Near-work-induced transient myopia.** This leftover near focus after close work runs **0.2–0.6 D** ([SUNY SOAR](https://soar.suny.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/5d18a289-4943-418b-82a7-6e6ea09916a3/content)).

Together these plausibly account for **0.5–1 D** of apparent "reversal" in a typical adult. That accounts for much of the registry's median, but not the multi-diopter success stories, which no documented mechanism explains. Controlled tests of Bates-style eye exercises found no significant change in refractive error ([Australian Orthoptic Journal](https://www.aojournal.com.au/archive/by-doing-eye-exercises-can-you-really-through-away-your-myopic-correction/)). A systematic review found "no clear scientific evidence" for eye exercises outside convergence insufficiency ([Rawstron et al. 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15825744/)).

The evidence on under-correction runs against Endmyopia's distance strategy, at least in children. In a Hong Kong RCT of 94 children, those under-corrected by about 0.75 D **progressed faster**: −1.00 D vs −0.77 D over two years ([Review of Myopia Management](https://reviewofmm.com/does-the-undercorrection-of-myopia-inhibit-myopia-progression/)). Adler and Millodot found a small, non-significant increase. The Logan and Wolffsohn meta-analysis concluded under-correction "should no longer be advocated" ([Aston](https://publications.aston.ac.uk/id/document/73481)). A retrospective US dataset (Vasudevan 2014) is cited by proponents as pointing the other way, but it is observational, and its direction was not confirmed in this research ([PMC4087177](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4087177/)). No adult under-correction RCT exists.

Endmyopia's near-only differential is closer to mainstream "less minus at near" ideas than its distance under-correction is. Mainstream myopia control, though, never claims reversal. Its best treatments **slow** progression in children by roughly 30–70%. Stellest lenses cut progression 67% in full-time wearers ([AOP](https://www.aop.org.uk/ot/news/2023/10/13/essilor-stellest-lenses-slow-down-myopia-progression-by-67)), and MiSight contact lenses slowed elongation about 50% over seven years ([CooperVision](https://coopervision.com/our-company/news-center/press-release/pivotal-international-seven-year-misight-1-day-contact-lens)). Outdoor time **prevents onset**: 40 extra minutes a day cut three-year incidence from **39.5% to 30.4%** ([He et al., JAMA 2015](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2441261)). No professional body endorses natural reversal. The 2023 WSPOS consensus lists under-correction as not recommended ([WSPOS](https://www.wspos.org/swdcore/uploads/WSPOS-Myopia-Consensus-Statement-2023-1.pdf)).

The method does have a legitimate foothold. The American Optometric Association's guideline says full minus lenses for pseudomyopia "may further stimulate the process" and should not be prescribed long term ([AOA guideline](https://www.aoa.org/AOA/Documents/practice%20Management/Clinical%20Guidelines/Consensus-based%20guidelines/Care%20of%20patient%20with%20myopia.pdf)). Endmyopia is right that some prescriptions are stronger than needed. Its error is extrapolating from that to structural reversal.

## Critics and users split, but the evidence does not

Professional criticism is consistent. There are no controlled trials. Progress is tracked with self-measured acuity rather than cycloplegic refraction or biometry. Small reversible effects are stretched into claims of structural change. And, as one Quora critic put it, "most of the papers cited by Jake don't say what he implies that they say" ([Quora](https://www.quora.com/If-EndMyopia-isnt-true-then-how-can-you-explain-the-improvements-that-people-are-reporting)). A general-audience explainer sums up the consensus: "you can't really train an eyeball to get shorter" ([Gromeus](https://www.gromeus.com/health/eyes/endmyopia-method-claims-reverse-myopia-science/)). A behavioral optometrist told Steiner his "understanding of vision is shockingly simplistic", in an exchange hosted on Endmyopia's own site ([Endmyopia](https://endmyopia.org/eye-doctor-your-understanding-of-vision-is-shockingly-simplistic/)). No named eye doctor's full debunk was located, and no regulatory or legal action was found. That is a gap in what was searched, not proof that none exists.

User reports are polarized. Trustpilot holds both "life changing" reviews and "scam" complaints. One reviewer reports about **0.75 D in three years**, and others say their vision worsened ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/endmyopia.org)). A Hacker News commenter claims "severe myopia" to near-normal ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=25914919)). Reddit could not be accessed, so community failure and dropout rates remain unresearched. Without a denominator of non-responders, the anecdotes cannot be weighed. They are consistent with a real but modest, mostly accommodative and perceptual effect, which the most enthusiastic users read as cure.

## Safe enough for careful adults, wrong for children

A balanced verdict separates the habits from the claims. Mild distance under-correction (0.25–0.5 D), reduced near lenses for screens, outdoor time and avoiding over-minus prescriptions carry **low physical risk for adults with low to moderate myopia**. Several overlap with mainstream advice, and they may shave off an accommodative component of a few tenths of a diopter to about a diopter. The method fails where it promises more than that, in four ways.

**It does not shorten the eye, so it does not reduce disease risk.** Retinal-detachment odds rise to **3.1× below −3 D, 9× at −3 to −6 D and 21.5× beyond −9 D** ([Review of Myopia Management – Flitcroft](https://reviewofmm.com/myopia-increase-the-risk-of-serious-sight-threatening-eye-disease/)). Lifetime visual-impairment risk climbs from **3.8% at axial length under 26 mm to 25% at 26 mm or more and about 90% at 30 mm or more** ([Tideman, JAMA Ophthalmology 2016](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaophthalmology/fullarticle/2569443)). These risks persist "even with appropriate refractive correction" ([Community Eye Health Journal](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6688422/)). A person who reads −4 D instead of −6 D after the method still has a −6 D-length eye.

**Driving below legal acuity is a real hazard**, especially with the wiki's −0.75 to −1.00 D normalized options.

**Children differ from adults.** Their eyes are still growing, under-correction showed harm in the best RCT, and proven alternatives exist.

**Self-tracking can mask disease.** Home charts mislead, and new flashes or blur fluctuations could be misread as "clear flashes". That last point is speculative, but it matters for long eyes.

Adults also keep progressing more than many assume. Roughly **20% of myopes aged 20–40 progress by at least 1 D over five years** ([PubMed 41249003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41249003/)), so a stable or drifting prescription needs proper measurement either way.

### Practical safety checklist

| ✓ | Step | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ☐ | **Keep a legal driving pair**: full correction for night driving, and for daytime driving whenever the normalized pair might fall below your jurisdiction's standard (UK 6/12 plus a 20 m number plate; many US states use about 20/40, so verify locally) | Endmyopia itself "strongly recommends" full correction at night; reduced lenses at −0.75 D under approach 20/40 ([AOP](https://www.aop.org.uk/advice-and-support/for-patients/driving-and-vision-standards); [Wiki – Night vision](https://wiki.endmyopia.org/wiki/Night_vision)) |
| ☐ | **Do not apply it to children without an eye doctor.** Ask instead about outdoor time, low-dose atropine, DIMS/HAL spectacles, MiSight or ortho-k | Under-correction sped progression in children ([Chung RCT summary](https://reviewofmm.com/does-the-undercorrection-of-myopia-inhibit-myopia-progression/)); proven control options exist |
| ☐ | **Get a baseline and follow-up cycloplegic refraction plus optical axial length** (e.g., IOLMaster) rather than relying on home Snellen and cm tests | Only this separates real change from blur adaptation, pseudomyopia release and over-minus ([Blur adaptation](https://researchconnect.suny.edu/en/publications/blur-adaptation-in-myopes/); [Pseudomyopia in adults](https://openarchive.usn.no/usn-xmlui/handle/11250/2725703)) |
| ☐ | **Keep yearly dilated retinal exams** (more often if highly myopic or axial length is 26 mm or more), with eye-pressure and optic-disc checks | Detachment, maculopathy and glaucoma risk follow axial length, not lens power ([CEHJ](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6688422/); [Tideman 2016](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaophthalmology/fullarticle/2569443)) |
| ☐ | **Seek same-day care for new floaters, flashes or a curtain over vision**; do not interpret them as "clear flashes" | Standard retinal-detachment warning signs (general clinical teaching) |

## Conclusion

Endmyopia's lasting contribution is to show how much of a measured prescription is negotiable. Accommodative excess, generous minus and blur tolerance can together account for up to about a diopter. A cautious adult can reclaim that by wearing slightly weaker distance lenses and screen-specific near glasses, at little cost or risk. The method's leap from there to reversing axial myopia now lacks support even from its own website. As long as no one runs the measurements that would settle the question (cycloplegic refraction and biometry on a defined cohort, including dropouts), the honest reading is that Endmyopia makes **glasses** weaker, not **eyes** shorter.

A practical consequence follows. Success on Endmyopia's own terms can increase risk if it is mistaken for cure. Someone whose prescription "drops" but whose eye is still 26 mm or more long has the same retinal risk as before, and possibly less motivation to keep up exams. Anyone trying the two-pair setup should treat axial length, not the number on their glasses, as the measure of eye health. They should also keep the full-strength pair for night driving and as a fallback that meets the legal standard for daytime driving.
