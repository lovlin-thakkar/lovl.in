# Scientific Evidence on Natural Myopia Reduction Claims (Endmyopia / Jake Steiner method), with Focus on Adults

Research note, current to early October 2026. Method: web search of the peer-reviewed literature and professional bodies. Note: PubMed Central, NCBI, myopiaprofile.com and reviewofoptometry.com could not be fetched from this environment (egress-blocked), so several figures come from search-result abstracts and snippets rather than full-text reads. These are flagged where it matters. Figures marked "(recall, unverified)" come from background knowledge and were NOT confirmed this session. The report writer should treat them as gaps, not facts.

## Q1. Is myopia mainly axial, can axial length shorten in humans, and how big are choroidal (defocus) effects?

### Takeaway
Most myopia comes from the eyeball growing too long (axial elongation). Imposed myopic defocus, the mechanism behind Endmyopia's "differential" and reduced lenses, does shorten measured axial length in humans. The effect is roughly 7–20 µm, it comes from the choroid thickening, and it fades within hours, which works out to about 0.02–0.05 D. Even the strongest axial "shortening" seen in research, from red-light therapy, is about 0.06 mm in adults over 4 weeks and is also mostly choroidal. No peer-reviewed study shows a lasting, clinically meaningful reversal of established axial myopia in adults.

### Cited Findings
- Read et al. measured an **8–13 µm decrease in axial length** after 40–60 minutes of +3 D myopic defocus, in both myopic and emmetropic young adults. Chiang et al. measured about **20 µm of choroidal thickening** after 60 minutes of +2 D myopic defocus in young adults. — [IOVS: Short-Term Myopic Defocus and Choroidal Thickness in Children and Adults](https://iovs.arvojournals.org/article.aspx?articleid=2793556)
- Time-course study (Sci Rep 2020): myopic defocus gave an average axial change of **−7 µm at 30 min**, and hyperopic defocus gave **+9 µm**. Both peaked at about **±10 µm at 60 min**, and the paper also tracked recovery after the defocus was removed (transient). — [Nature Sci Rep 2020: time course of onset and recovery of axial length changes](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-65151-5)
- Defocus also changes choroidal thickness in schoolchildren, and astigmatic defocus causes short-term choroidal changes in adults. These are short-term effects only. — [PMC4990278](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4990278/); [PMC7425733](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7425733/)
- Optical biometers (IOLMaster) measure "axial length" from the cornea to the RPE (retinal pigment epithelium). A thicker choroid therefore shows up as a "shorter" eye even though the sclera (outer wall) has not changed. — [Myopiaprofile: Is axial length regression possible?](https://www.myopiaprofile.com/articles/is-axial-length-regression-possible) (search snippet; full text not fetchable)
- Work on these short-term defocus responses is still active in 2026, for example how the response varies over the day and how it differs across refractive groups. Exact magnitudes were not retrieved. — [ScienceDirect 2026: Variations of axial eye shortening in response to positive defocus over the day](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S188842962600021X); [Vision Research 2026: Short-term axial eye length changes after imposed defocus in emmetropes, myopes and hyperopes](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0042698926000283)
- Adult red-light RCT (2022): **98 myopic adults** (52 RLRL, 46 control), 3-minute sessions twice daily for 4 weeks. Axial length fell from **24.63 ± 1.04 to 24.57 ± 1.04 mm (−0.06 mm)** and subfoveal choroidal thickness rose by **+18.34 µm**. — [Axial Length Shortening and Choroid Thickening in Myopic Adults Treated with RLRL (PMC9780890)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9780890/) (abstract-level data via search)
- In children, RLRL produced axial shortening of more than 0.05 mm in **59%** of the treated group vs **0%** of controls at 12 months. Rebound elongation after stopping treatment is documented. — [RLRL update on efficacy and safety (PMC13431227)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13431227/); [ScienceDirect 2026 review: RLRL rebound, safety boundaries](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1572100026002218)
- Endmyopia's own site appears to concede that "sustained reversal of established adult axial myopia is not established in the literature", while pointing to short-term axial changes and animal recovery data as plausibility arguments. — [endmyopia.org: Can myopia be reversed?](https://endmyopia.org/can-myopia-be-reversed/) (attribution via search summary; not fetched directly)

### Inferences
- Conversion: using the common rule of thumb that 1 mm of axial length ≈ 2.5–3 D of refraction (standard optics rule; no source retrieved this session), 10–20 µm ≈ **0.03–0.06 D** and 0.06 mm ≈ **~0.15–0.18 D**. Both are below the 0.25 D step of a standard prescription. The task brief's "tens of microns ≈ ~0.1 D" is the right order of magnitude, slightly generous.
- Defocus-driven choroidal thickening is real and is the scientific kernel behind Endmyopia's "differential" and reduced lenses. But the effect is small, fades within hours, and comes from choroidal thickness, not scleral remodeling. Any lasting gains of several dioptres claimed by Endmyopia users are therefore unlikely to come from axial shortening. More likely sources are accommodative or pseudomyopic components, blur adaptation, measurement variability, or over-minus in the original prescription. This is inference; no controlled study has partitioned the claimed gains.
- In chicks and other animals, myopia induced by blur or lenses recovers when the blur is removed. This is the "recovery in juveniles" that Endmyopia cites. Those are young, still-growing eyes with experimentally induced myopia, so the data do not transfer to adult human axial myopia.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed long-term (≥1 year) study shows durable axial shortening in adults from optical defocus alone.
- It is unclear whether the RLRL-induced axial "shortening" includes any true scleral change beyond choroidal thickening. The literature debates this and it was not resolved here.

## Q2. Controlled studies of Endmyopia, "active focus", "clear flashes", pseudomyopia and accommodative contributions in adults

### Takeaway
No controlled or peer-reviewed trial of the Endmyopia protocol, or of reduced-lens plus "active focus" regimes, was found for adults (or for anyone). Endmyopia's evidence is a self-run, unblinded case registry. Better-studied phenomena explain most "improvements": blur adaptation improves uncorrected letter acuity without changing refraction, NITM and pseudomyopia are typically 0.2–0.6 D, and in one adult sample about 7% showed meaningful pseudomyopia. Natural-vision and Bates-style exercises have failed in controlled testing.

### Cited Findings
- **No Endmyopia RCT found.** Endmyopia publishes a "Case Report Registry" of **363 cases** with start and end diopters, **61** of which carry "third-party or official-record signal". These are self-selected, unblinded and not peer-reviewed. — [Endmyopia Wiki: Clinical Studies](https://wiki.endmyopia.org/index.php?mobileaction=toggle_view_desktop&title=Clinical_Studies); [endmyopia.org](https://endmyopia.org/can-myopia-be-reversed/)
- "Clear flashes" have **no peer-reviewed study under that name**. Endmyopia attributes them to ciliary-muscle (focusing-muscle) relaxation, framed as NITM or "spasm". — [Endmyopia: Simeon, Clear Flashes to 20/15](https://endmyopia.org/simeon-clear-flashes-2015-vision/) (proponent source)
- Tear-film quality measurably affects the optical quality of the eye. Irregular tear film adds higher-order aberrations, and artificial tears that smooth the film reduce them. This makes a tear-film "lens" a physically plausible contributor to transient clarity changes, but it has not been tested as an explanation for clear flashes. — [Vision 2024: The Influence of Tear Film Quality on Visual Function](https://doi.org/10.3390/vision8010008); [ResearchGate: The Tear Film and the Optical Quality of the Eye](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/47518783_The_Tear_Film_and_the_Optical_Quality_of_the_Eye)
- **Blur adaptation:** 22 young myopes (mean −1.85 D) went **3 hours without correction**. Uncorrected letter acuity improved from **0.76 to 0.53 logMAR** (about 6/35 → 6/20), with **no significant change in refractive error** on noncycloplegic autorefraction. The authors attribute this to perceptual (cortical) adaptation. Pesudovs & Brennan (1993) also found reduced blur perception after time without glasses. — [SUNY: Blur adaptation in myopes (Rosenfield group)](https://researchconnect.suny.edu/en/publications/blur-adaptation-in-myopes/); [Healio 2004: blur adaptation may occur in visual cortex](https://cm.healio.com/news/ophthalmology/20120331/blur-adaptation-may-occur-in-visual-cortex)
- **Pseudomyopia in young adults (Norway, ages 18–40):** about **7%** met criteria for pseudomyopia (cycloplegic autorefraction vs noncycloplegic subjective refraction). Noncycloplegic autorefraction read on average **−0.43 ± 0.37 D** more myopic, and **40%** of subjects read ≥0.50 D more myopic without cycloplegia. — [USN Open Archive: Pseudomyopia and NITM in young Norwegian adults](https://openarchive.usn.no/usn-xmlui/handle/11250/2725703)
- A smaller regional study of 200 adult patients reported accommodative spasm in **19%**. Its quality is uncertain (regional journal). — [Tishreen/Latakia Univ. journal: prevalence of accommodative spasm in adults](https://journal.latakia-univ.edu.sy/index.php/hlthscnc/en/article/view/11946)
- **NITM** (nearwork-induced transient myopia, the leftover near focus after close work) measures about **0.2–0.6 D** and decays back to baseline within roughly **6–68 seconds**. In one myopic sample it averaged **0.35 D**, and adult myopes show larger and longer-lasting NITM than emmetropes. A proposed (unproven) link is that time-integrated NITM contributes to permanent myopia. — [SUNY SOAR: NITM following sustained accommodation](https://soar.suny.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/5d18a289-4943-418b-82a7-6e6ea09916a3/content); [Aston: NITM](https://publications.aston.ac.uk/id/eprint/4622)
- **Natural Vision Therapy (Bates-style) controlled trial:** despite highly motivated subjects, there were **no statistically significant changes in refractive error** vs control. Year and details were not retrieved. — [Australian Orthoptic Journal: "By Doing Eye Exercises Can You Really Throw Away Your Myopic Correction"](https://www.aojournal.com.au/archive/by-doing-eye-exercises-can-you-really-through-away-your-myopic-correction/)
- Rawstron, Burley & Elder (2005), a systematic review in J Pediatr Ophthalmol Strabismus 42(2):82–88, found **"no clear scientific evidence"** for eye exercises outside convergence insufficiency. Myopia was among the conditions reviewed. — [PubMed 15825744](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15825744/)
- Bates' central premise, that the eye focuses by being squeezed longer by the extraocular muscles, is physiologically false. The method was never accepted by ophthalmology. — [EBSCO Research Starter: Bates method](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/health-and-medicine/bates-method)
- Accommodative-facility training ("active focus"-like flipper training) improves accommodative dynamics and symptoms in myopes and emmetropes. That is a different outcome from reducing refractive error. — summary via [search results; underlying paper not identified]

### Inferences
- Several mechanisms can produce real, reproducible gains in uncorrected **acuity** (Snellen lines) without any change in axial length: blur adaptation, NITM and pseudomyopia release, tear-film changes, squinting or pinhole effects, chart memorization, and over-minus in the starting prescription. The first two are well documented. Endmyopia's progress metric is mostly self-measured acuity on charts, so this metric is exactly the one these confounders affect.
- The size of the reversible accommodative component in a typical non-cycloplegically refracted adult is plausibly **~0.25–0.5 D on average** (USN data), and larger in a minority. A subjective refraction that "pushes minus" can bake this into a prescription. Dropping that excess could plausibly look like "reversing" 0.5–1 D. Losses of 3 D or more on Endmyopia claims are not explained by any documented mechanism.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study of "clear flashes" was found.
- No study has measured cycloplegic refraction and axial length before and after an Endmyopia-style protocol.
- No large population estimate of pseudomyopia prevalence in adults outside the Norwegian and Syrian samples was found.

## Q3. Undercorrection: does it slow, speed or not affect progression?

### Takeaway
Randomized trials in children found that undercorrection either has no effect or speeds progression. A systematic review and meta-analysis (Logan & Wolffsohn) concluded undercorrection should no longer be advocated. One retrospective US clinical dataset (Vasudevan et al. 2014) found the opposite association, and proponents cite it, but it is observational. There is essentially no controlled adult data.

### Cited Findings
- **Chung, Mohidin & O'Leary 2002 RCT** (94 Hong Kong children aged 9–14, 2 years): undercorrected children progressed **faster**, about **0.50 D/yr vs 0.38 D/yr** fully corrected (≈ −1.00 D vs −0.77 D over 2 yrs). The undercorrection amount was about +0.75 D (recall, unverified). — [Review of Myopia Management](https://reviewofmm.com/does-the-undercorrection-of-myopia-inhibit-myopia-progression/); [Vision Science Academy](https://visionscienceacademy.org/is-under-correcting-myopia-helpful-to-prevent-myopia-progression/)
- **Adler & Millodot 2006 RCT** (48 children aged 6–15, undercorrected by +0.50 D, 18 months): progression was **0.17 D greater** with undercorrection. The difference was not statistically significant. — [Review of Myopia Management](https://reviewofmm.com/does-the-undercorrection-of-myopia-inhibit-myopia-progression/)
- **Logan & Wolffsohn systematic review and meta-analysis** (Clin Exp Optom, about 2020; 8 prospective cohorts plus 1 retrospective analysis, ages ≤18): results conflicted. Most studies showed **equal or faster** progression with undercorrection, and the review concluded undercorrection "should no longer be advocated". — [Aston Publications: Role of un-correction, under-correction and over-correction](https://publications.aston.ac.uk/id/document/73481); [Review of Myopia Management summary](https://reviewofmm.com/role-of-un-correction-undercorrection-and-overcorrection-of-myopia-as-a-strategy-for-slowing-myopic-progression/)
- **Vasudevan et al. 2014** (retrospective US clinical records), "Under-correction of human myopia: is it myopigenic?": proponents cite it as showing that greater undercorrection is associated with slower progression. It is observational and contrary to the RCTs. — [PMC4087177](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4087177/) (direction of the finding from recall; full text not fetched, so verify)
- Graefe's 2015 paper on undercorrection in 12-year-old children (Anyang cohort, observational): its direction was not verified this session. — [Springer PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00417-015-3053-8.pdf)
- The WSPOS 2023 Myopia Consensus Statement lists undercorrection among strategies not recommended. Wording not retrieved verbatim. — [WSPOS Consensus 2023](https://www.wspos.org/swdcore/uploads/WSPOS-Myopia-Consensus-Statement-2023-1.pdf)
- Hong Kong Children Eye Study: undercorrected children had altered habitual reading distance, a behavioral side effect relevant to near-work load. — [Springer 2022: Effect of myopic undercorrection on habitual reading distance](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40123-022-00628-2)

### Inferences
- Endmyopia's "differential" for near work is a hybrid: full or near-full minus is reduced specifically at near, which is closer to "plus at near" and progressive-addition-lens strategies. Those were tested in children (e.g., the COMET PAL trial) and produced only small effects (recall, unverified). Endmyopia's general distance undercorrection ("normalized" lenses) is the version the RCTs above argue against.
- Adults progress slowly, so even a harmful or beneficial effect of undercorrection would be hard to detect in adults without large trials.

### Gaps
- No RCT of undercorrection in adults was found.
- No trial of Endmyopia-style "differentials" (reduced minus at near only) in adults was found.

## Q4. Outdoor time, light, near work, 20-20-20: prevention vs reversal

### Takeaway
The evidence is strong that more outdoor time **prevents or delays onset** in children, cutting incidence by roughly a quarter to a third in RCTs. The effect on **progression** in children who are already myopic is weaker, and there is no evidence that outdoor time or the 20-20-20 rule **reverses** established myopia in adults.

### Cited Findings
- **He et al. 2015 JAMA cluster RCT** (Guangzhou, grade 1): adding 40 minutes of outdoor activity at school cut 3-year cumulative myopia incidence from **39.5% to 30.4%** (about 23–25% relative reduction). — [JAMA](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2441261); [PMC6688404](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6688404/)
- **Wu et al. 2018 Ophthalmology** (Taiwan cluster RCT): ≥11 hours per week outdoors was associated with about a **30% reduction in onset**. Light intensity mattered. — [Ophthalmology 2018;125:1239–1250 via search](https://www.aaojournal.org/article/S0161-6420(22)00483-3/fulltext)
- An overview of systematic reviews (2022) and a meta-analysis of outdoor time vs SE and axial length (2025) found evidence strongest for **prevention of onset**. Exact progression effect sizes were not retrieved. — [PMC9305934](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9305934/); [PMC12604451](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12604451/)
- Childhood outdoor time is associated with lower odds of myopia in adulthood. — [Sci Rep 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-85825-y)
- The AAO Task Force (Modjtahedi et al., Ophthalmology 2021) found outdoor time to be the most-studied modifiable factor, aimed at **reducing onset**. — [Healio summary](https://www.healio.com/news/ophthalmology/20201124/increasing-myopia-a-growing-public-health-concern); [AAFP 2021](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2021/0401/p394.html)
- IMI 2025: delaying onset by even 1 year may give greater lifetime benefit than several years of progression control. — [mivision IMI 2025 updates](https://mivision.com.au/2025/10/imi-2025-white-paper-updates/)

### Inferences
- The proposed mechanisms for outdoor protection are bright light driving retinal dopamine release, and more distant viewing. These act on the growth signal of a still-elongating eye. In an adult whose eye has stopped growing, there is no ongoing elongation for them to slow, so outdoor time cannot be expected to shorten an already-long eye.
- The 20-20-20 rule is reasonable for comfort and asthenopia (eye strain), but no trial found shows that it reduces refractive error.

### Gaps
- No RCT of the 20-20-20 rule on refraction or axial length was found.
- No trial of outdoor time for adult myopia progression was found.

## Q5. Proven myopia CONTROL treatments: effect sizes, and which work in adults

### Takeaway
Several interventions reliably **slow** axial elongation in children, typically by 30–70% relative to single-vision lenses. None of them reverse myopia, and nearly all evidence is pediatric. Adult myopia control is being trialled (for example Stellest in ages 18–40) but is not established. Red-light therapy shows the largest effects but has unresolved safety concerns.

### Cited Findings
- **Low-dose atropine, LAMP (Hong Kong):** in year 2, axial growth was **0.19 mm** with 0.05%, **0.23 mm** with 0.025% and **0.24 mm** with 0.01%. 0.05% was the most effective concentration, and 9.1% of children on 0.05% still progressed by more than 2 D over 2 years. Long-term LAMP results now extend to 8 years. — [IOVS: LAMP Phase 2](https://iovs.arvojournals.org/article.aspx?articleid=2747191); [Review of MM: 8-year LAMP](https://reviewofmm.com/eight-year-results-from-the-lamp-trial/); [AAO EyeNet: low-dose atropine](https://www.aao.org/eyenet/article/low-dose-atropine-for-myopia-control)
- **CHAMP phase 3** (0.01%/0.02%, US/Europe): at 36 months axial length grew **0.81 mm** on placebo, **0.68 mm** on 0.01% and **0.73 mm** on 0.02%, a modest effect. There was **no meaningful rebound** after switching to placebo in year 4. — [Optometry Times ARVO 2024](https://www.optometrytimes.com/view/champ-phase-3-trial-finds-myopia-controlled-by-low-dose-atropine-0-01-over-4-year-course); [PMC13369339](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13369339/)
- **DIMS (MiyoSmart) over 6 years:** children who wore it throughout progressed **−0.92 ± 1.15 D** with **0.60 ± 0.49 mm** of axial elongation, with sustained control and no adverse effects. — [Sci Rep 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-32700-7)
- **HAL (Stellest):** in full-time wearers (≥12 h/day) over 2 years, myopia was slowed by **0.99 D (67%)** and axial elongation by **0.41 mm (60%)** vs single-vision lenses. — [AOP 2023](https://www.aop.org.uk/ot/news/2023/10/13/essilor-stellest-lenses-slow-down-myopia-progression-by-67); [Myopia Institute: JAMA Ophthalmology 2-year](https://myopiainstitute.org/jama-ophthalmology-publishes-two-year-clinical-trial-results-of-essilors-stellest-lens/)
- **MiSight 1 day:** over 7 years, axial elongation was slowed by about **50%** across ages 8–17, with **no rebound** 12 months after stopping. — [CooperVision press release](https://coopervision.com/our-company/news-center/press-release/pivotal-international-seven-year-misight-1-day-contact-lens); [Optometry Times](https://www.optometrytimes.com/view/misight-1-day-contact-lenses-show-no-rebound-effect-in-children-new-data-reveals)
- **Orthokeratology:** a 2-year trial found progression reduced by **67%** with ortho-k and **43%** with a peripheral-gradient soft lens, vs single vision. — [Pauné et al. (PMC4641166)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4641166/)
- **Repeated low-level red light (RLRL):** RCTs show significant slowing of axial elongation, with network meta-analyses ranking it above other interventions, and axial shortening in many children. Rebound occurs after stopping. — [PMC13431227](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13431227/); [AJO 2024 RCT in high myopia](https://www.ajo.com/article/S0002-9394(24)00481-1/fulltext); [Ophthalmology 2024](https://www.aaojournal.org/article/S0161-6420(24)00318-X/fulltext)
- **RLRL safety:**
  - A 2023 JAMA Ophthalmology case report described a 12-year-old whose acuity fell from 20/20 to 20/30 after 5 months, with foveal ellipsoid-zone disruption. — [Ophthalmology Times](https://www.ophthalmologytimes.com/view/commercial-red-light-devices-for-myopia-may-exceed-safety-limits); [Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/amp/ophthalmology/news/safety-concerns-raised-over-some-repeated-low-level-red-light-therapy-devices-for-myopia-control-jama-176293)
  - Ostrin & Schill (University of Houston, 2026) tested 4 commercial devices (Sky-n1201, Future Vision, EyeRising, AirDoc) against ANSI Z80.36-2021 and Z136.1-2022 and found they **exceeded safety limits**. They called for independent safety validation. — [Optometry Times, Feb 2026](https://www.optometrytimes.com/view/some-red-light-therapy-devices-may-exceed-safety-levels-for-myopia-control-study-suggests); [UH News, Mar 2026](https://www.uh.edu/news-events/stories/2026/march/03022026-ostrin-red-light-myopia-research-needed.php)
  - Other reports describe decreased cone density and OCT changes in about **7%** of children. — [Ophthalmology Times](https://www.ophthalmologytimes.com/view/commercial-red-light-devices-for-myopia-may-exceed-safety-limits)
  - A systematic review gives an adverse-event rate of 0.088 per 100 patient-years, with no irreversible loss in trials. — [PMC13431227](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13431227/); [ScienceDirect safety SR](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2162098924001440)
- **Adults:**
  - **Progression rates.** Myopic adults aged 18–25 elongate about **0.05–0.10 mm/yr** (mean 0.07). Roughly **20%** of myopic adults aged 20–40 progress by ≥1.00 D over 5 years. In a population cohort of high myopes, the share progressing fell from **26.6% at 18 to 3.8% at 21**, with none at ages 22–25. Faster elongation is associated with axial length ≥28 mm, female sex and age <40. — [IMI: Onset and Progression of Myopia in Young Adults (IOVS 2023)](https://iovs.arvojournals.org/article.aspx?articleid=2785578); [PMC13217049 / PubMed 41249003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41249003/); [AJO 2024 high-myopia young adults](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0002939424003593); [JAMA Ophthalmol: elongation trajectories in high myopia](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaophthalmology/fullarticle/2813169)
  - **Trials in progress.** A Stellest RCT in adults aged 18–40 who are still progressing is registered (NCT06520124), as is a focused-LED red-light trial in myopic adults (NCT07369804). Results were not found. — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06520124](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06520124); [NCT07369804](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07369804)
- **IMI 2025** white paper "Interventions for controlling myopia onset and progression" groups interventions into optical, pharmacological, environmental/behavioral, coloured-light and surgical (experimental) categories. Only RCTs were used to assess established interventions. — [Review of MM: IMI 2025](https://reviewofmm.com/imi-releases-2025-white-paper/); [IMI 2025 Digest (PMC12448141)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12448141/)

### Inferences
- The best-supported treatments slow progression by roughly 0.3–1.0 D over 2–3 years in children. That is small relative to the multi-dioptre "reversals" claimed by Endmyopia users. Professional-grade interventions with strong evidence do not claim reversal.
- Ortho-k gives daytime clear vision by temporarily flattening the cornea. It is not a cure, and the refractive effect wears off when lenses are stopped (standard knowledge; no source retrieved). It is the only non-surgical route to substantially better uncorrected acuity in adults.
- Adult eyes progress slowly, so the absolute benefit of myopia control in adults is likely small. It may matter most in high myopes still elongating (axial length ≥28 mm).

### Gaps
- LAMP Phase 1 1-year figures (recall, unverified; verify before use): SE progression −0.27/−0.46/−0.59 D for 0.05/0.025/0.01% vs −0.81 D placebo, and AL 0.20/0.29/0.36 vs 0.41 mm.
- Original DIMS 2-year efficacy (recall, unverified): about 52% (SE) and 62% (AL).
- MiSight 3-year efficacy (recall, unverified): about 59% (SE) and 52% (AL).
- The US regulatory status of low-dose atropine (CHAMP/Vyluma) as of 2026 was not verified.
- No completed adult myopia-control RCT results were found.

## Q6. What professional bodies say about reversal, "natural cure", eye exercises and Bates

### Takeaway
No major professional body (AAO, IMI, WSPOS) endorses natural reversal of myopia. Their guidance focuses on prevention through outdoor time and on slowing progression in children with atropine and optical treatments. The systematic-review evidence on eye exercises is negative. Explicit, quotable AAO, AOA or WHO statements that specifically address "Endmyopia" or reversal claims were not retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- **AAO Task Force on Myopia** (Modjtahedi, Abbott, Fong, Lum, Tan; Ophthalmology 2021) frames myopia as a public-health problem. Its recommendations centre on outdoor time to delay onset and on low-dose atropine (0.01–0.1%, endorsed in the 2017 AAO Ophthalmic Technology Assessment) for control. It does not mention reversal. — [Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/ophthalmology/20201124/increasing-myopia-a-growing-public-health-concern); [AAFP 2021](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2021/0401/p394.html); [Review of Optometry: AAO launches global myopia task force](https://www.reviewofoptometry.com/news/article/academy-of-ophthalmology-launches-global-myopia-task-force)
- **IMI 2025** describes a shift from passive correction toward risk identification, prevention, early intervention and personalized control. It contains no reversal claims. — [Review of MM](https://reviewofmm.com/imi-releases-2025-white-paper/); [AOP overview](https://www.aop.org.uk/ot/videos/2025/07/22/an-overview-of-the-imi-2025-whitepapers)
- **WSPOS 2023 consensus**: management through outdoor time, atropine and optical interventions; undercorrection not advised. — [WSPOS PDF](https://www.wspos.org/swdcore/uploads/WSPOS-Myopia-Consensus-Statement-2023-1.pdf)
- **Eye exercises:** besides Rawstron 2005 (see Q2), meta-analyses of "Chinese eye exercises" (the school massage routine) report weak or no protective association. — [PMC10036375](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10036375/); [Frontiers Public Health 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2023.950700/full); [medRxiv 2023 meta-analysis](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.01.29.23284986.full.pdf)

### Inferences
- The professional consensus implicitly rejects reversal of axial myopia. The emphasis is on slowing progression because elongation is considered irreversible in practice.

### Gaps
- Verbatim AAO patient-education ("EyeSmart") or AOA statements on eye exercises or Bates were not retrieved, although AAO public pages are widely reported to say exercises do not correct refractive error.
- WHO material: the WHO's 2015 report "The impact of myopia and high myopia" and the World Report on Vision (2019) exist (recall), but no WHO statement on natural cures was retrieved.
- No professional-body statement naming Endmyopia was found.

## Q7. Risks of high myopia, and why axial length matters regardless of prescription

### Takeaway
Risk of sight-threatening complications rises steeply with myopia level and, more fundamentally, with **axial length**. Reducing the glasses prescription through lens strategies, adaptation or refractive surgery does **not** change axial length, so it does not reduce these structural risks.

### Cited Findings
- **Flitcroft 2012** (pooled odds ratios):
  - Retinal detachment: OR **3.1** at < −3 D, **9.0** at −3 to −6 D and **21.5** at worse than −9 D.
  - Even −1 D roughly doubles the risk of myopic maculopathy and posterior subcapsular cataract and triples retinal-detachment risk.
  - Myopia roughly doubles the risk of open-angle glaucoma.
  - Sources: [Review of MM](https://reviewofmm.com/myopia-increase-the-risk-of-serious-sight-threatening-eye-disease/); [Myopiaprofile: the why of myopia control](https://www.myopiaprofile.com/articles/the-why-of-myopia-control)
- **Haarman et al. 2020 IOVS** meta-analysis of myopia complications confirms graded risk. — [IOVS](https://iovs.arvojournals.org/article.aspx?articleid=2765517)
- **Tideman et al. 2016 JAMA Ophthalmology** (Europeans): lifetime risk of visual impairment is **3.8%** with axial length <26 mm, **25%** at ≥26 mm and about **90%** at ≥30 mm. Eyes longer than 28 mm carry roughly **11–24×** the risk of eyes shorter than 24 mm. — [JAMA Ophthalmology](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaophthalmology/fullarticle/2569443); [Myopiaprofile summary](https://www.myopiaprofile.com/articles/association-of-axial-length-with-risk-of-uncorrectable-visual-impairment-for-europeans-with-myopia)
- Myopic retinal sequelae and glaucoma are associated in a global cohort. — [PMC12523566](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12523566/)
- Axial length vs refractive error: clinicians increasingly track axial length because it drives the risk. — [Review of MM](https://reviewofmm.com/axial-length-vs-refractive-error/)

### Inferences
- Someone who "reduces" a −6 D prescription to −4 D through adaptation, pseudomyopia release or wearing undercorrected lenses still has the same eyeball length, so their retinal-detachment and maculopathy risk is unchanged. Regular dilated fundus exams remain essential. This point matters for any write-up because Endmyopia's framing implies that health risk falls along with the prescription.
- Possible harms of the method itself:
  - Driving or working with deliberately undercorrected distance vision raises safety concerns. Legal driving standards apply; no data was retrieved on this.
  - Symptoms of retinal detachment could be misattributed as "clear flashes" or blur fluctuations. This is speculative and flagged as such.

### Gaps
- No study was found measuring adverse outcomes, such as accidents or delayed diagnoses, among Endmyopia practitioners.
