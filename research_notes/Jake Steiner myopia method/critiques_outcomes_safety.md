## 1. What eye-care professionals and science communicators say about Endmyopia

### Takeaway
Mainstream eye care has not endorsed Endmyopia. The main criticisms: no randomized controlled trial (RCT) has shown adults reversing established axial myopia; Steiner's citations reportedly don't support his claims; his model of vision is oversimplified; and an elongated eyeball does not shorten through training. Even Endmyopia's own newer pages now concede there is no RCT evidence. I found no prominent named optometrist or ophthalmologist debunk (blog or YouTube) in the search results. That is a gap, not proof that none exists.

### Cited Findings
- A general-audience explainer says "most eye doctors and scientists say it's not that simple." Once the eyeball grows too long "it usually stays that way," and "you can't really train an eyeball to get shorter." It also notes that "no randomised controlled trial has shown an adult reversing established myopia this way." — [Project Gromeus: "Endmyopia claims to reverse nearsightedness naturally (but science remains skeptical)"](https://www.gromeus.com/health/eyes/endmyopia-method-claims-reverse-myopia-science/)
- One critic on Quora says that "most of the papers cited by Jake don't say what he implies that they say" (critic's identity and credentials not verified). — [Quora: "If EndMyopia isn't true, then how can you explain the improvements…"](https://www.quora.com/If-EndMyopia-isnt-true-then-how-can-you-explain-the-improvements-that-people-are-reporting); also [Quora: "Is endmyopia.org's information true?"](https://www.quora.com/Is-endmymyopia-orgs-information-true-Can-myopia-really-be-reversible)
- A behavioral optometrist told Steiner his "understanding of vision is shockingly simplistic" and that vision and myopia are more complex than he describes. The exchange is hosted on Endmyopia's own site, so it is framed by Steiner. — [Endmyopia: "Eye Doctor: 'Your Understanding Of Vision Is Shockingly Simplistic'"](https://endmyopia.org/eye-doctor-your-understanding-of-vision-is-shockingly-simplistic/); see also [Endmyopia: "In Response to Jake's Most Extreme Anti-Optometry Blog So Far"](https://endmyopia.org/response-jakes-extreme-anti-optometry-blog-far/)
- Endmyopia's own site states that its advice is "100% unlicensed, unsanctioned, un-endorsed by the optometry establishment." — [Endmyopia: About Jake Steiner](https://endmyopia.org/about-us/) (via search snippet)
- Steiner's framing is adversarial toward optometry. In a podcast he called optometry "a $100 billion industry that feeds on people's ignorance" and claimed retail optometrists call myopia "mysterious and genetic" even though academia understands its causes. — [MelanieAvalon.com podcast with Jake Steiner](https://melanieavalon.com/myopia/)
- Endmyopia's own wiki has a "Frequently Asked Questions/Skepticism" page and the site has a "Does EndMyopia Work?" page, which shows the project responds to skepticism directly. — [Endmyopia Wiki: FAQ/Skepticism](https://wiki.endmyopia.org/wiki/Frequently_Asked_Questions/Skepticism); [Endmyopia: Does EndMyopia Work?](https://endmyopia.org/does-endmyopia-work/)
- A secondary summary notes that "the details of Jake Steiner's research on his clients are not publicly shared or published" (source page not identified in the snippet; treat with caution). — via search summary associated with [Endmyopia Wiki: FAQ/Skepticism](https://wiki.endmyopia.org/wiki/Frequently_Asked_Questions/Skepticism)
- On pseudomyopia, the American Optometric Association (AOA) clinical guideline says pseudomyopia comes from overstimulated accommodation. Prescribing minus lenses for it "may further stimulate the process," and the full manifest minus power should not be prescribed long term. This partly overlaps with Endmyopia's argument, but it applies to pseudomyopia, not axial myopia. — [AOA Optometric Clinical Practice Guideline: Care of the Patient with Myopia](https://www.aoa.org/AOA/Documents/practice%20Management/Clinical%20Guidelines/Consensus-based%20guidelines/Care%20of%20patient%20with%20myopia.pdf)
- Endmyopia claims that distance blur "started as pseudomyopia" (a ciliary spasm) and that you "didn't need glasses" at that stage. — [Endmyopia: How Do You Improve Myopia? The Behavioral Approach](https://endmyopia.org/how-do-you-improve-myopia/)
- Physiological basis cited by both sides: Read et al. (2010) found the human choroid thickens under myopic defocus, shortening measured axial length, and thins under hyperopic defocus. These changes are about 10 microns over 60 minutes, far too small to explain diopter-scale reversal (roughly 0.3–0.4 mm of axial length per diopter is the usual optics rule of thumb; that conversion is my own and is not from the snippet). — [PMC: Short-term peripheral myopic defocus (Fresnel lenses) study, citing Read 2010](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8608875/)
- Endmyopia's own page addresses the axial question: "Can An Elongated Eyeball Be Reversed? What The Science Says." — [Endmyopia: axial change science](https://endmyopia.org/axial-change-myopia-science/) (content not read)

### Inferences
- The critiques that hold up best: (a) no controlled trials; (b) outcomes are tracked mainly with self-measured Snellen acuity and self-reported prescriptions, not cycloplegic refraction or axial length; (c) real but small, reversible effects (pseudomyopia relaxation, choroidal thickening of tens of microns, blur adaptation) are extrapolated into claims of structural reversal; (d) the citations are allegedly misrepresented.
- Pseudomyopia gets clinical recognition (AOA), and outdoor time works for prevention (see section 5). Endmyopia builds on these legitimate points, which makes it harder to debunk wholesale than the Bates method.

### Gaps
- I could not find, in snippets, a specific named optometrist or ophthalmologist YouTube video or blog debunking Endmyopia. Candidates the writer could check manually: Myopia Profile, Review of Myopia Management, and eye-doctor YouTubers. None was confirmed here.
- I found no AAO or AOA patient page that names Endmyopia.
- I could not verify the content or authorship of the Quora critiques.

## 2. User-reported outcomes (Reddit, forums, testimonials, registry)

### Takeaway
User reports range from no change to dramatic claims (for example, "severe myopia to 20/20"). Endmyopia's own numbers say followers gain about 0.75–1 D per year, with roughly 1.5–1.75 D median reduction in its self-reported registry. Only a small minority of cases (about 42–61 depending on the snippet) have any third-party verification, and that is mostly ordinary optometrist prescriptions or driving-test passes, not cycloplegic refraction. The one axial-length case found shows about 0.1 mm, which is within the range of choroidal and measurement variability. No controlled data on dropout or failure exist.

### Cited Findings
- **Endmyopia Case Report Registry** (self-published, CC BY 4.0 CSV). Snippets give conflicting figures:
  - One version: "466 published reports documenting 289 distinct cases… 2013–2026, with 42 cases having third-party or official-record signal… median improvement of 1.5 D/year over 12+ month cases." — [Endmyopia: Case Report Registry](https://endmyopia.org/myopia-improvement-registry/)
  - Another version from the same domain: 363 documented cases, of which 61 carry third-party or official-record verification (49 optometrist or ophthalmologist exam results, 12 driving-licence restrictions lifted). — [Endmyopia: Case Report Registry](https://endmyopia.org/myopia-improvement-registry/) and [Endmyopia: How Do You Improve Myopia?](https://endmyopia.org/how-do-you-improve-myopia/)
  - "363 adult cases… median starting prescription of around −5.00 D and median reduction of around −1.75 D. Sixty-one… include third party verification from optometrists with no connection to EndMyopia." — [Endmyopia: Can myopia be reversed? What the evidence actually says](https://endmyopia.org/can-myopia-be-reversed/)
  - The registry concedes it is "self-reported… subject to selection and survivorship bias, since people who improve are more likely to write back. It does not prove that the method reliably reverses established adult myopia." — [Endmyopia: Case Report Registry](https://endmyopia.org/myopia-improvement-registry/)
  - Each case records "start and end diopter numbers as stated by the person" and is graded by verification class, from self-measured up to independent confirmation. — [same](https://endmyopia.org/myopia-improvement-registry/)
- Claimed typical rate: about 0.75–1 D per year for diligent followers, "not weeks." — [Endmyopia: How Do You Improve Myopia?](https://endmyopia.org/how-do-you-improve-myopia/); [Endmyopia: Can myopia be reversed?](https://endmyopia.org/can-myopia-be-reversed/)
- **Axial length:** one Endmyopia-featured user reported an axial-length reduction of 0.08 mm (left eye) and 0.10 mm (right eye) over about one year. The device, cycloplegia status and repeatability were not available in the snippet. — [Endmyopia: "Axial Length Reduced: Biometry Results"](https://endmyopia.org/axial-length-reduced-biometry-results/)
  - For context, myopic defocus can thicken the choroid and reduce measured axial length by tens of microns in the short term ([PMC8608875, citing Read 2010](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8608875/)), so a 0.08–0.10 mm change cannot be attributed to scleral or structural shortening without repeated, controlled measurements.
- Trustpilot reviews are mixed. One reviewer reports about 0.75 D improvement over three years and notes it took 20 years to develop the prescription. Another credits the community with keeping a teenage son out of glasses. Others call the site "scammy" or a scam. — [Trustpilot: Endmyopia reviews](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/endmyopia.org)
- A Hacker News commenter wrote "Endmyopia is real. It works, and I'm not a shill. I went from severe myopia to 2…" (anecdote; the full text was not readable). — [Hacker News item 25914919](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=25914919)
- Endmyopia publishes curated "Optometrist Confirmed" and success-story posts, such as "Chris: 25% Better Eyesight, Optometrist Furious," "Eva: My Optometrist Knows About Endmyopia!" and "Ophthalmologist: 'Your Program Works!'" These are selected testimonials on the vendor's own site. — [Category: Optometrist Confirmed](https://endmyopia.org/category/optometrist-confirmed/); [Chris](https://endmyopia.org/chris-25-better-eyesight-optometrist-furious/); [Eva](https://endmyopia.org/eva-my-optometrist-knows-about-endmyopia-and-lots-of-gains-reports/); [Ophthalmologist](https://endmyopia.org/ophthalmologist-your-program-works/)
- Endmyopia's own explainer says cycloplegic drops reveal pseudomyopia: if myopia mostly disappears under cycloplegia, it was pseudomyopia, not axial myopia. — [Endmyopia: pseudomyopia vs axial myopia](https://endmyopia.org/what-is-the-difference-between-pseudomyopia-and-axial-myopia/)

### Inferences
- The registry's own numbers put verified cases at about 12–21% of cases (42/289 ≈ 15%, 61/363 ≈ 17%). "Verified" usually means a later non-cycloplegic prescription or a passed driving test. Neither rules out pseudomyopia relaxation, accommodative changes or measurement variability.
- The median starting point is about −5 D and the median drop about −1.75 D. That fits with removing an accommodative or over-correction component, and possibly some real change, but it does not demonstrate axial reversal. The vendor's own explainer gives the test that would settle it (cycloplegic refraction plus biometry), yet that test is apparently rare in the registry.
- Survivorship bias is large. Non-responders and dropouts have no structured record, and the registry acknowledges this.

### Gaps
- I could not access Reddit (r/myopia, r/endmyopia, r/myopiaFree), so I have no quantified Reddit failure or dropout stories or typical Reddit-reported gains. The writer should treat Reddit sentiment as unresearched here.
- No independent study (academic or journalistic) of Endmyopia outcomes was found.
- No case was found with repeated, cycloplegic, optical-biometry-documented axial length reduction beyond the single ~0.1 mm Endmyopia-hosted case.
- The registry figures conflict (363/61 vs 289/42). They may be different snapshots or definitions. This needs direct verification on the page or CSV.

## 4. Safety considerations and safe-practice guidance

### Takeaway
The main risks are: (1) driving or working below legal acuity with reduced lenses; (2) for children, deliberate undercorrection, which was linked to faster progression in an RCT (Chung 2002), although later trials were mixed; (3) skipping exams. Myopia raises the risk of retinal detachment, glaucoma and myopic maculopathy based on eye length, and wearing weaker lenses does not lower that risk. Anyone trying this should keep a legal-to-drive correction, keep regular (dilated) exams, and track progress with cycloplegic refraction and axial length rather than home eye charts.

### Cited Findings
**Undercorrection evidence**
- Chung et al. (2002) ran an RCT with 94 children aged 9–14. Undercorrecting by +0.75 D (to about 6/12) *increased* progression: −1.00 D vs −0.77 D over 24 months, with axial elongation of 0.7 vs 0.6 mm. — [Review of Myopia Management: Does undercorrection inhibit progression?](https://reviewofmm.com/does-the-undercorrection-of-myopia-inhibit-myopia-progression/); [Myopia Profile: myopia correction in children](https://www.myopiaprofile.com/articles/myopia-correction-children)
- Adler & Millodot (2006) found a slight, non-significant increase in progression with undercorrection. Li (2015) found no difference between under- and full correction. Vasudevan (2014) studied −0.50 D undercorrection in ages 11–33 (its direction of effect was not confirmed in the snippet). A review concluded trials show a non-significant overall effect, with myopigenic effects reported when undercorrection exceeds about 1.5 D. — [PubMed 31854025: Role of un-, under- and over-correction](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31854025/); [PubMed 16907670: Adler & Millodot](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16907670/); [encyclopedia.pub summary](https://encyclopedia.pub/entry/1814)
- Myopia Profile publishes guidance for clinicians on "communicating with parents who insist on undercorrection," which shows the profession sees this as a real clinical issue. — [Myopia Profile](https://old.myopiaprofile.com/?p=31031)

**Disease risk unaffected by lens power**
- "Even with appropriate refractive correction," myopia increases the risk of open-angle glaucoma, retinal tears and detachment, and myopic maculopathy. Glaucoma risk is about 50% higher in moderate-to-high myopia than in low myopia, and retinal detachment risk is five to six times greater in high myopia, because stretched retinas and degenerate vitreous are prone to tears. — [Community Eye Health Journal: High myopia and its risks (PMC6688422)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6688422/)
- Recommended care: the search summary attributes to the AAO a recommendation that moderate or high myopes have comprehensive exams at least yearly, with dilated retinal evaluation, eye-pressure measurement and imaging when indicated. CEHJ also recommends checking IOP and optic disc at each visit. — [Community Eye Health Journal](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6688422/); [HealthXchange (Singapore): avoiding blindness from high myopia](https://www.healthxchange.sg/health-articles/how-to-manage/eye-care/how-to-avoid-blindness-caused-by-high-myopia). (I could not fetch the AAO page itself, so treat the exact AAO wording as unverified.)

**Driving / legal acuity**
- In the UK (DVLA), drivers must read a number plate at 20 metres and have acuity of at least 6/12 (decimal 0.5, about 20/40) with correction if needed. Bus and lorry drivers need 6/7.5 in the better eye and 6/12 in the other. — [Association of Optometrists: Driving and vision standards](https://www.aop.org.uk/advice-and-support/for-patients/driving-and-vision-standards); [College of Optometrists / AOP principles](https://www.college-optometrists.org/Category-landing-pages/Driving/principles-for-vision-standards-for-driving-uk)
- The DVLA says it cannot license drivers who report being advised that their vision is below 6/12. — [Optician Online](https://www.opticianonline.net/content/news/snellen-test-standard-to-rely-on-honesty)
- Endmyopia's own verification class counts "a passed DMV or driving-licence vision test" as evidence. — [Endmyopia registry](https://endmyopia.org/myopia-improvement-registry/)

**Pseudomyopia caveat (supports a narrow version of the method)**
- The AOA guideline says full minus correction should not be prescribed long term for pseudomyopia. — [AOA guideline](https://www.aoa.org/AOA/Documents/practice%20Management/Clinical%20Guidelines/Consensus-based%20guidelines/Care%20of%20patient%20with%20myopia.pdf)

### Inferences (practical safe-practice checklist the writer can use, with the basis for each)
- **Keep a separate, fully legal driving correction.** Many US states use roughly 20/40 as a common threshold (not verified state by state here), and the UK uses 6/12 plus the 20 m number plate. Reduced "differentials" should never drop below this when driving.
- **Children: don't self-undercorrect.** The best RCT (Chung 2002) showed harm, later trials were null, and evidence-based myopia-control options exist (outdoor time, plus clinician-prescribed atropine, DIMS/HAL spectacles or orthokeratology, which other researchers cover). Undercorrection beyond about 1.5 D was described as myopigenic.
- **Measure properly.** Ask for cycloplegic refraction to separate pseudomyopia from axial myopia, and optical biometry (axial length, e.g., IOLMaster or Myopia Master) at baseline and follow-up. Home Snellen charts are affected by lighting, chart memorization and blur adaptation.
- **Keep dilated retinal exams.** Retinal detachment, glaucoma and maculopathy risk depend on axial length, and lowering lens power does not change them. Learn the detachment warning signs (new floaters, flashes, curtain), which are standard clinical teaching though not directly sourced here.
- **Stop or reassess** with persistent headaches, eye strain, double vision, or any sudden vision change. Headaches and eyestrain from undercorrection are widely reported but I did not source them specifically.

### Gaps
- I did not verify US state-by-state driving acuity rules (commonly 20/40, but this varies).
- I found no published data on headaches or eyestrain rates among Endmyopia users.
- I found no documented case of harm (missed detachment or glaucoma) attributable to Endmyopia.
- The direction of Vasudevan 2014's finding is unclear from the snippet (I recall it reported less progression with more undercorrection, but did not confirm it here).

## 5. Related low-risk, evidence-based habits vs Endmyopia claims

### Takeaway
Outdoor time has strong RCT evidence for *preventing or delaying myopia onset in children*: an extra 40 minutes a day of outdoor time cut 3-year incidence from 39.5% to 30.4%. Near-work breaks, lighting and avoiding over-correction are reasonable but less proven. These habits are about prevention and slowing progression. Endmyopia's claim is reversal of established adult myopia, which is a separate claim without trial support.

### Cited Findings
- He et al., JAMA 2015: a cluster RCT across 12 Guangzhou schools with grade-1 children aged 6–7 (n=919 intervention, n=929 control), 2010–2013. A compulsory 40-minute outdoor class daily cut 3-year cumulative myopia incidence to 30.4% vs 39.5% in controls, about a 23% relative reduction. — [ANU repository: He et al. 2015](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/items/6ac1bf5b-77fa-4d50-9d61-18bbe056d93e/full); [HealthDay](https://healthday.com/healthpro-news/exercise-and-fitness/more-outdoor-time-at-school-may-help-prevent-myopia-in-children-703236.html); [UTS OPUS PDF](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/43515/3/MED26733109%20am.pdf)
- Endmyopia also recommends more natural light and outdoor time, together with gradual prescription reduction and "active focus." — [Trustpilot summary / Endmyopia materials](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/endmyopia.org); [Endmyopia: Active Focus](https://endmyopia.org/active-focus-does-it-actually-work-clinical-science/)
- AOA guidance: avoid prescribing full minus for pseudomyopia, so avoiding over-minus correction is consistent with mainstream practice. — [AOA guideline](https://www.aoa.org/AOA/Documents/practice%20Management/Clinical%20Guidelines/Consensus-based%20guidelines/Care%20of%20patient%20with%20myopia.pdf)
- Optical interventions to slow progression have been reviewed systematically, separately from undercorrection. — [PMC6688415: Optical interventions to slow the progression of myopia](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6688415/)

### Inferences
- The overlap between Endmyopia and mainstream care (outdoor time, near-work breaks, not over-minusing, relaxing pseudomyopia) is the low-risk, evidence-aligned part. The departure is the claim that lens reduction plus "active focus" reverses axial elongation in adults, and the de-emphasis on professional monitoring.
- A reader can adopt the habits without the reversal claims or the paid course.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced evidence for the 20-20-20 rule's effect on myopia progression (search returned nothing specific).
- I did not source evidence on lighting levels or near-work distance in adults.
- No trial has tested "active focus" specifically.
