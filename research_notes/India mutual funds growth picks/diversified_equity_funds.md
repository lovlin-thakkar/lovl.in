## 1. Market backdrop and passive benchmarks (Nifty 50 / Next 50 / Midcap 150), end-Sep 2026

### Takeaway
2026 has been a bad year for Indian large caps. The Nifty 50 was down about 11.5% YTD by late September 2026 and fell 6.1% in September alone, closing at 22,620.45. Nifty 50 index funds show only about 5.6–6% CAGR over 3 and 5 years. The Nifty Next 50 (about 16% 3-yr) and Midcap 150 (about 16.7% 3-yr and 5-yr to Aug-2026) beat it by a wide margin. This sets a low large-cap bar for active funds to clear, and a much higher mid-cap bar.

### Inferences
- PPFAS's lower trailing returns in 2026 go together with clearly better drawdown behaviour. Its holdings in foreign big-tech stocks, its cash and debt sleeve (see section 5), and its value-oriented holdings explain both. For a growth ranking it is the lowest-volatility core option, not the highest-return one.
- Motilal Oswal LMC's positive 1-yr return in a year when the Nifty 50 fell about 8% is notable. However, Motilal Oswal funds are concentrated, momentum-style portfolios, and Motilal Oswal Large Cap and Multi Cap fell harder (about −10%) than PPFAS in the 2024–25 correction. No drawdown figure for Motilal Oswal LMC itself was found.
- In September 2026, Kotak (−5.73%) and Franklin (−5.96%) fell about as much as the market, so they gave no meaningful downside protection.
